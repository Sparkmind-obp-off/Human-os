# Human OS — Safety, Privacy & Trust

## Core Principles
- Least privilege
- Explicit consent
- Reversible actions where possible
- Human approval for consequential actions
- Secret isolation
- Auditability
- Data minimization
- User-controlled memory

## High-Risk Actions
Examples include:
- financial transactions
- deleting important data
- publishing externally
- sending sensitive communications
- changing security settings
- actions affecting legal/medical/financial outcomes

These require explicit confirmation or a pre-defined policy with appropriate safeguards.

## Dependency Guardrails
Human OS should support connection without encouraging exclusivity. It should not say or imply that the user only needs the AI, that real people are unnecessary, or that the AI has a special claim over the user.

## Emergency/Sensitive Situations
When a user appears to face immediate danger or severe distress, prioritize safety-oriented guidance and appropriate human/professional support rather than positioning Human OS as the sole support.

## Kill Switch
The user must be able to pause autonomous execution and revoke connector permissions.
