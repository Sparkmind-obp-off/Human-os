# Human-OS Brand Naming — Cycle 005

**Methodology:** Acquisition-Led Brand Recovery / Existing-Asset First
**Cycle date:** 2026-09-18
**Status:** NO BRAND LOCK — Strategy Shift Recommended
**Scope discipline:** All claims labelled by evidence class per §5 of the Cycle 005 master prompt (`VERIFIED` / `SEARCH-OBSERVED` / `UNVERIFIED` / `REQUIRES PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE`).

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Cycle 005 tested a specific hypothesis:

> Can Human-OS obtain a commercially viable, defensible brand identity more efficiently by **acquiring or recovering an existing brand/domain asset** than by generating another new invented name?

The evidence gathered in this cycle does not support that hypothesis in the form it was posed. Every asset class that was researched — Google-retired product brands, acqui-hired independent AI startups, open-source voice-assistant projects, and dormant SaaS identities — was disqualified by at least one Hard Gate under §13 of the master prompt. In parallel, the third asset class considered by the prompt — *premium brandable domain marketplaces* (Atom.com, Sedo, BrandBucket, Flippa) — was found to be a live, priced, credible market, but it is a **domain-acquisition** path, not a **brand-recovery** path, and it does not remove the professional trademark-clearance step that Cycles 001–004 already identified as the true blocking constraint.

The correct decision under §21 (No-Forced-Winner Rule) is:

- **Decision:** NO BRAND LOCK.
- **Outcome class (per §3 of the master prompt):** OUTCOME 4 — STRATEGY SHIFT RECOMMENDED.
- **Shortlist size:** 0 acquisition candidates fully pass all mandatory pre-screens. 3 candidates are documented as *instructive near-misses* to justify the strategy shift, not to be locked.
- **Strongest evidence gaps:** WIPO / USPTO / EUIPO / DJKI direct searches remain UNVERIFIED for every candidate examined, exactly as recorded in Cycle 004. This cycle did not close that gap; a licensed trademark professional must.
- **Professional trademark clearance still required:** YES — mandatory before any Cycle 006 candidate is locked.

The most important finding of Cycle 005 is diagnostic rather than nominative:

> The blocking constraint on the Human-OS brand is not the *supply* of candidate names. It is the absence of an authoritative trademark-clearance pipeline inside the naming loop. Cycles 001–004 tried to solve this by generating better names; Cycle 005 tried to solve it by finding recoverable names; both are the wrong lever. Cycle 006 must solve it by inserting a professional clearance channel before any candidate reaches lock stage.

## 2. Cycle Objective

Determine whether Human-OS can obtain a defensible new commercial brand by starting from **existing brand/domain assets and acquisition opportunities**, and select only assets that can plausibly survive a rigorous commercial, linguistic, ecosystem, domain, and trademark pre-screen — without inventing another name.

The task was explicitly *not* to run another invented-name generation cycle (per §0 and §2 of the master prompt).

## 3. Product Understanding (grounded in repository evidence)

Extracted directly from `docs/01_PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md` in the repository ([raw.githubusercontent.com](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sparkmind-obp-off/Human-os/main/docs/01_PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md)):

- **Product:** "Human OS is a persistent, voice-first personal operating layer for reasoning, coordination, memory, and action."
- **Core loop:** Sense → Understand → Plan → Act → Verify → Remember → Reflect (per the repository README, [github.com](https://github.com/Sparkmind-obp-off/Human-os)).
- **North Star (verbatim):** *"I can call Human OS and say what I need; it understands the context, coordinates the right tools, performs safe actions, and brings important decisions back to me."*
- **Autonomy levels:** L0 Explain → L1 Suggest → L2 Prepare → L3 Execute reversible → L4 Pre-authorised workflows with monitoring → L5 Never autonomous (policy-gated).
- **Capability domains include:** personal organization, work/project coordination, research, communication drafting, calendar/task coordination, file retrieval, business-system orchestration, browser/tool execution, health-adjacent organization (without pretending to be professional care), reflection and planning.
- **Anti-personas (from the naming constraints already established in `docs/20`):** the brand must not read as *fake human persona*, *emotional companion*, *feature name*, *chatbot*, or *toy*.

The naming constraints locked in the repository ([Cycle 001 `docs/20`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sparkmind-obp-off/Human-os/main/docs/20_HUMAN_OS_BRAND_NAMING_RESEARCH_AND_LOCK.md)) remain in force for Cycle 005:

1. No `AI`, `OS`, `Agent`, `Bot`, `Automation`, `Assistant` in the name.
2. Premium, calm, technically serious — not toy-like, gimmicky, crypto-like.
3. Speakable in Indonesian and English without correction.
4. Scales from single-user tool to enterprise workspace without renaming.
5. Not a persona / friend / companion name.
6. Not a feature name (Voice, Copilot, Assistant, Buddy, Helper).
7. Short enough for "Call [NAME]." and long enough to be distinctive.
8. A stranger typing the name into Google must not land in a dominant unrelated brand.

These eight constraints are the objective screen every candidate below is measured against.

- **Cycle 004** ([`docs/24`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sparkmind-obp-off/Human-os/main/docs/24_HUMAN_OS_BRAND_NAMING_CYCLE_004.md)) — ~130 raw, 20 in deep screen, 13 end-to-end. Cycle 005 is a **methodological break**: instead of generating candidates, it asks whether the market already contains a viable brand/domain asset that Human-OS can obtain.

Per §22 of the master prompt, the bounded conclusion from four generation cycles is:

> Four independent generation-oriented cycles have produced repeated collision pressure in the tested naming spaces. This is evidence against continuing the same generation strategy, but it does not establish that the global naming space is exhausted.

Cycle 005 tests the *supply-side alternative*.

### 5.3 Territory C — Acquirable short domains

Independent of any historical brand, short pronounceable `.com` / `.ai` domains are listed for sale on the Atom / Sedo / BrandBucket marketplaces, with prices and lease-to-own terms as documented in §5.1. This is a real pathway. Its constraint is not availability; its constraints are (a) trademark clearance, still required per Cycle 004 evidence gaps, and (b) the marketplace does not sell historical goodwill — it sells a name string.

### 5.4 Territory D — Historical brands with clean semantic distance

The theoretical target here is a name whose *prior* meaning is genuinely unrelated to AI, personal OS, productivity, voice interaction, autonomy, or developer platforms, but which is nevertheless commercially recoverable. In practice, the intersection between "recoverable" and "clean semantic distance" is extremely narrow, because most attractive historical brands in commerce are still held by successor entities or by defensive trademark filings after acquisition (see §5.2). No Territory D candidate cleared the pre-screen.

Selection heuristics per §4 of the master prompt:

1. Prefer short pronounceable strings that clear both Indonesian and English phonotactic quick-tests.
2. Reject any candidate with obvious active trademark or brand-search collision under the Cycle 001 §8 rule.
3. Reject any candidate whose prior use produces reputational contamination.
4. Reject any candidate that reads as a persona, feature, or toy per Cycle 001 §5–6.
5. Preserve candidates where the *only* remaining objection is "ownership path unclear" — treat these as UNKNOWN, not FAIL, per §13.

Approximately 30 raw brand strings entered the initial candidate pool. After the four rejection heuristics, three names remained worth documenting as instructive near-misses. None fully cleared the mandatory gates.

## 11. Linguistic & Cultural Screen

Brief tests against the eight-constraint block from Cycle 001 (§20).

- **Timeful.** English: pronounced /ˈtaɪm.fəl/, easy; but it reads as a *dictionary construction* (`time` + `-ful`), which resembles a feature-descriptor pattern the Cycle 001 rule §6 explicitly bans ("must not read as a feature name"). Consistent with §21: no weak candidate has been backfilled. The result is a valid research outcome, not a failure.

Explicit residual gaps, mandatory to record per §5 and §6:

1. **WIPO Global Brand Database** — not queried directly in Cycle 005. Same limitation as Cycle 004.
2. **USPTO TSDR / TESS** — not queried as first-party in Cycle 005. Trademarkia was used as a secondary index for one live filing.

Prices for specific candidates in Territory C are `PRICE = UNKNOWN` unless the operator opens a purchase negotiation directly with the broker.

## 18. Legal / Professional Clearance Requirements

The following steps cannot be discharged by another naming cycle and must be performed by qualified professionals before any brand is locked:

1. First-party trademark search in USPTO (Nice classes 9 and 42), EUIPO, DJKI Indonesia, and WIPO Global, for any candidate proposed by Cycle 006.
2. Domain-history and WHOIS retrieval for the top three candidates entering Cycle 006, ideally via a domain broker with drop-catch and negotiation capability (Sedo brokerage, Saw.com, or equivalent).
3. Freedom-to-use opinion in Indonesia (DJKI-anchored) and in at least one major English-speaking common-law jurisdiction (US, UK, or Australia) — this is the operator's primary launch context.
4. Independent linguistic clearance for at least Bahasa Indonesia, English, Spanish, Portuguese, Mandarin, Arabic, and Japanese, performed by a specialised naming firm rather than by a generalist agent.
5. If a Territory-C marketplace domain is chosen, a purchase agreement that explicitly assigns any residual trademark rights the seller holds, and that indemnifies against prior use.

Every one of these steps is standard trademark-clearance workflow. None of them was performed in Cycle 005.

## 19. Strategic Learning

The compounded evidence across Cycles 001–005 supports four learnings, stated in bounded language (§22):

1. **The blocking constraint is not name supply.** Four generation cycles and one acquisition cycle have failed for reasons unrelated to name inventory. Cycle 002 failed on hyper-saturation of *categories*, Cycle 003 on adversarial phonetic collision at the *individual* level, Cycle 004 on cross-jurisdictional multilingual and business collision, and Cycle 005 on ownership and reputation contamination in the acquisition market. A sixth generation cycle would be expected to fail on the same or an adjacent axis.
2. **The blocking constraint is authoritative clearance capability.** Every cycle has terminated on trademark or business-collision evidence that a licensed practitioner can generate reliably and an automated agent cannot. Cycle 004 stated this explicitly in its evidence-limitations section; Cycle 005 reproduced the same gap on a different candidate set.
3. **The acquisition market is real for domains and narrow for brands.** Atom / Sedo / BrandBucket / Flippa constitute a live, priced market for domain strings. There is no comparable open market for recovering trademark-active retired product brands from institutional holders like Google, Meta, Samsung, or Apple. The "brand recovery" hypothesis of Cycle 005 is therefore substantially narrower in practice than in theory.
4. **Persona-adjacent inheritance is the hidden risk of Territory B.** Every attractive Google-retired candidate that survived initial filtering — Doppl, Fabric, Areo, Timely — reveals on closer inspection a persona-adjacent or feature-adjacent posture that Cycle 001 §5–6 already ruled out. Acquisition does not solve the anti-persona constraint; it only relocates it.

---

## 20. Recommendation for Cycle 006

Cycle 006 should be **the first cycle that does not start with a candidate list**. Its opening move must be structural, not lexical:

### 20.1 Engage a professional naming and clearance channel

Rather than adding a sixth internal cycle, Cycle 006 should retain, in this order:

1. A licensed trademark attorney with practice in Indonesia (DJKI) and at least one Madrid Protocol jurisdiction (USPTO or EUIPO).
2. A domain broker with observed marketplace inventory access — Sedo brokerage, Atom brand strategist tier, or Saw.com are documented commercial options ([sedo.com](https://sedo.com/us/), [atom.com](https://www.atom.com/)).
3. A specialised naming firm capable of running Bahasa Indonesia and English adversarial linguistic screens as a paid service, with a short list of no more than 20 candidates as input.

The internal repository work then becomes brief-writing and gate-checking, not name generation.

### 20.2 Use Territory C, not Territory B

If a fresh candidate list is generated at all, it should be sourced from **existing curated marketplace inventory** (Atom / BrandBucket / Sedo), not from the graveyard indexes. This inverts Cycle 005's implicit assumption: the marketplace is designed to sell pre-cleared brandable names; the graveyard is not designed to sell recovered brands. Prior evidence supports treating the marketplace as the primary supply channel.

### 20.3 Insert clearance *before* shortlisting, not after

The single change most likely to unblock the loop is placing the professional trademark search **inside the pre-screen**, not after the lock decision. Cycles 001–005 consistently produced shortlists that then failed clearance. Cycle 006 should produce candidates that have already cleared, so that the internal screens (linguistic, architectural, adversarial) become the *final* filter rather than the *first*.

### 20.4 Explicitly defer, do not silently revert

Per §24 of the master prompt: "If acquisition fails, do not secretly revert to another invented-name cycle. Recommend the next methodology explicitly."

Cycle 006 is recommended to be a **professional-clearance-first cycle**, not another generation cycle in a new territory.

---

## Cycle 005 principle, restated

> **Acquire before inventing. Verify before attaching meaning. Clear before locking.**

Cycle 005 tested the first clause. Cycles 006 must operationalise the second and third.
