# 4. ACQUISITION TERRITORIES

Research at least these territories.

## A. Premium domain + brand packages

Search legitimate marketplaces and broker listings for:
- short pronounceable invented words;
- abstract technology brands;
- infrastructure/platform names;
- dormant SaaS identities;
- names with clean phonetics in Indonesian and English.

Prioritize assets where:
- domain and brand identity are aligned;
- seller/listing appears real;
- transfer appears commercially plausible;
- historical use is limited or compatible;
- the asset is not associated with harmful, adult, gambling, crypto-scam, malware, political-extremist, or other problematic categories.

Do not treat a marketplace listing as proof of trademark ownership.

## B. Dormant / inactive technology brands

Find brands that:
- existed as a software, SaaS, developer, infrastructure, productivity, or technology business;
- appear inactive, abandoned, acquired, renamed, or commercially dormant;
- have an identity potentially compatible with Human-OS;
- may have a transferable domain or corporate/brand asset.

Distinguish:
- dormant website;
- inactive company;
- expired domain;
- abandoned trademark;
- available domain;
- available trademark.

These are NOT equivalent.

## C. Acquirable short domains

Search for:
- .com first;
- then credible technology-relevant TLDs only where commercially sensible;
- short, pronounceable names;
- no obvious person/place/dictionary collision;
- no obvious active software brand collision.

Do not optimize for domain length alone.

## D. Historical brands with clean semantic distance

Look for existing names whose previous meaning/use does not conflict with:
- AI;
- personal operating systems;
- productivity;
- software infrastructure;
- voice interaction;
- autonomous digital execution;
- developer platforms.

The previous business can be unrelated only if the identity is commercially recoverable and the old association is not harmful or confusing.

# 5. SOURCE DISCIPLINE

For every candidate, record evidence separately.

Allowed evidence labels:

- **VERIFIED** — directly confirmed from an authoritative source.
- **SEARCH-OBSERVED** — observed in public search results.
- **UNVERIFIED** — source could not be reliably checked.
- **REQUIRES PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE** — legal/trademark determination remains outstanding.

Never write:
- "trademark clear";
- "legally available";
- "safe to register";
- "no trademark conflict";

unless a genuinely authoritative/legal source establishes that fact.

A domain being available does NOT mean the trademark is available.

A company being dissolved does NOT mean its trademark rights are gone.

A trademark being expired in one jurisdiction does NOT prove global availability.

---

# 6. TRADEMARK / OWNERSHIP RESEARCH

For each serious candidate, investigate where accessible:

- WIPO Global Brand Database;
- USPTO;
- EUIPO;
- Indonesia DJKI / PDKI;
- UK IPO when relevant;
- relevant national trademark offices based on prior use;
- company registries;
- domain registration/history evidence where available.

Also determine:
- current owner;
- historical owner;
- relevant goods/services;
- jurisdiction;
- status;
- filing/registration dates;
- whether the mark appears abandoned, expired, cancelled, active, or uncertain.

Do not overstate legal conclusions.

If a source is inaccessible, explicitly record the gap.

---

# 7. DOMAIN RESEARCH

For each candidate:

Check, where technically possible:
- exact .com;
- relevant TLDs;
- current website;
- redirect history;
- apparent owner;
- marketplace listing;
- historical use;
- suspicious parked-domain behavior;
- cybersquatting indicators.

Do not claim WHOIS verification when WHOIS was not actually checked.

Record:

`DOMAIN_STATUS = AVAILABLE / ACQUIRABLE / ACTIVE_OWNER / PARKED / UNKNOWN / CONFLICT`

# 8. COMMERCIAL HISTORY SCREEN

Every candidate must be investigated for previous identity.

Check:
- company use;
- product use;
- startup use;
- software use;
- GitHub;
- npm;
- PyPI;
- app stores;
- social accounts;
- archived web presence where available;
- press/news;
- review sites where relevant.

Classify prior use:

1. Clean / minimal
2. Compatible
3. Ambiguous
4. Strong association
5. Disqualifying

Do not eliminate a candidate merely because any historical use exists.

The question is whether the prior identity creates a material confusion, reputation, ownership, or clearance problem.

# 13. HARD GATES

A candidate is eliminated if there is strong evidence of:

- active directly competing software brand;
- obvious active trademark conflict in relevant software/service territory;
- unavailable ownership with no credible acquisition path;
- serious reputation contamination;
- severe multilingual negative meaning;
- severe pronunciation/spelling ambiguity;
- obvious person/place identity likely to create material confusion;
- domain identity controlled by an unrelated active business with no acquisition path;
- inability to establish a plausible acquisition/ownership path.

"Unknown" is not automatically "fail."

Unknown must remain **UNKNOWN** and trigger further research or professional review.

# 17. FINAL BRAND LOCK RULE

Brand lock is permitted only if:

- exactly one candidate has a clearly documented acquisition path;
- ownership/transfer path is credible;
- trademark pre-screen does not reveal a material obvious conflict;
- domain identity is commercially coherent;
- linguistic/cultural screen is acceptable;
- Human-OS brand architecture works;
- adversarial review does not expose a disqualifying issue;
- remaining legal questions are explicitly documented for professional clearance.

Even then:

**BRAND LOCK = STRATEGIC LOCK, NOT LEGAL CLEARANCE.**

State clearly that final legal/trademark clearance remains a professional/legal step.

If those conditions are not met:

**NO BRAND LOCK.**
